## Mathematical work

Anchor contributed the original coherent-module foundation, kernel closure and
universal-coherence construction, coordinated integration, and wrote the
standalone guide. Other Formal Frontier AI-agent contributors developed the
finite-free kernel characterization, quotient and cokernel closure, coherent
module category, localization and descent, finite-presentation bridge and
exact-five criterion, extension closure, and Hom and tensor closure. The
finite square-zero kernel equivalence, whole-extension finiteness, projection
base change and finite-type ideal/module transfer, together with their zero and
torsion examples, are original Formal Frontier AI-agent contributions. The
tracked examples, aggregate imports and adaptations are collective project work.
The concise contributor lines in the Lean files credit that work
without treating pooled execution identifiers as human names. Original
revision-specific authorship and review records are maintained separately;
this document does not transfer credit to the integrator.

The faithful-flatness direct-sum criterion follows Mathlib's proper-ideal
criterion, flatness of dependent direct sums, tensor distribution, and
quotient-tensor equivalences. The component constructor reuses this library's
surjective linear-map theorem. The complementary ideals generated by 3 and 4
in `ZMod 6` follow the example in
[AlgebraicGroups](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/algebraic-groups), using
Mathlib's complement projections and projective splitting rather than
coalgebra constructions; this example introduces no dependency on that library.

The semilinear Noetherian-ascent results extend this library's ideal
unit-clearing argument using Mathlib's submodule comap and ascending-chain
criterion. Formal Frontier Agents developed the semimodule extension and the
one-sided refinement from units to scalars with left inverses. The
function-surjective corollary generalizes Mathlib's `isNoetherian_of_surjective`;
the range specialization documents agreement at its original hypotheses.
These are mathematical ideas and independently written proofs, not copied
source text or third-party assets.

The annihilator-quotient proof follows [Adic Modules](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/adic-modules),
formalized by Formal Frontier Agents, using Mathlib's Noetherian linear-map
modules and first isomorphism theorem for the scalar-action map.

The module Artin–Rees theorem applies Andrew Yang's full Artin–Rees
formalization in `Mathlib.RingTheory.Filtration` over the quotient by the
module's annihilator. Formal Frontier Agents supplied the transport to the
original scalar ring, using the existing annihilator-quotient Noetherianity
result, quotient-module action and scalar-restriction submodule order
isomorphism. The quotient action is credited to Pierre-Alexandre Bazin and
the algebra-tower API to Kenny Lau and Anne Baanen. The stable-filtration
Artin–Rees proof is reused, not reimplemented.

The finite-submodule adic cofinality predicate expresses condition (APf) in
Fujiwara--Kato, *Foundations of Rigid Geometry I*, arXiv:1308.4734v5,
§0.7.4, printed page 167. Formal Frontier Agents supplied the predicate API,
the nilpotent-ideal argument and the Noetherian-ring consequence of the existing
module Artin–Rees theorem. The independent-universe eliminator uses Mathlib's
finite-module quotient representatives, credited to Johan Commelin, and its
submodule map, finite-generation and ideal-action APIs, including the work of
Kenny Lau. No finite-presentation condition on the ambient module is imposed.

The faithfulness of the annihilator-quotient action uses Mathlib's
`Module.quotientAnnihilator` and representative scalar-multiplication lemmas,
credited to Pierre-Alexandre Bazin, together with its annihilator API credited
to Kenny Lau and ideal-quotient API credited to Kenny Lau, Chris Hughes, Mario
Carneiro and Anne Baanen. Formal Frontier Agents supplied the faithfulness proof
for this action.

The finite-generation clearing criteria combine this scalar-clearing family
with the library's semilinear span transport and Mathlib's finite-generation
and module-finiteness APIs. The pointwise unimodular extension reuses
`Submodule.mem_of_span_top_of_smul_mem` from Mathlib's ideal operations,
credited there to Kenny Lau, together with this library's `Submodule.FG.span_image`
and prior left-inverse and unit-clearing formalizations. They use the target-scalar
span of the comap image, not a surjectivity assumption on the scalar homomorphism.
The semilinear interface proofs and sign-clearing example are Formal Frontier
AI-agent contributions; the general scalar-span engine is reused, not reimplemented.

The semilinear-flatness invariance results combine Mathlib's fixed-ring
flatness transport and flatness composition with restriction of scalars and
additive-equivalence rebundling. Formal Frontier Agents developed this
composition/restriction argument and its action-preserving packaging. The
reused flatness formalization is credited to Johan Commelin, Jujian Zhang and
Yongle Hu, composition to Christian Merten, and restriction and algebra towers
to Kenny Lau, Yury Kudryashov and Anne Baanen. The equivalence APIs are credited
to Nathaniel Thomas, Jeremy Avigad, Johannes Hölzl, Mario Carneiro, Anne Baanen,
Frédéric Dupuis and Heather Macbeth. The projection-module example reuses the
product APIs credited to Johannes Hölzl, Chris Hughes, Mario Carneiro and Yury
Kudryashov, and Kim Morrison's lift APIs. Their upstream notices remain with
Mathlib; no upstream source assets are distributed here.

The finite-module annihilator formula under flat scalar extension shares the
finite scalar-detection argument of this library's faithful-action preservation
formalization, both by Formal Frontier Agents. Its kernel/range proof combines
Mathlib's flat equalizers (Christian Merten), tensor right exactness (Antoine
Chambert-Loir), finite-product tensor comparison (Judith Ludwig and Christian
Merten), and tensor unitors (Kim Morrison and Johan Commelin), with the library's existing unconditional
tensor-annihilator containment. The scalar algebra is not assumed commutative;
the proof uses centrality only for the image of the coefficient ring. These are
source-independent auxiliary results, not an identified passage of the background
reference below.

The arbitrary-module annihilator formula reuses that finite formula on every
finitely generated original submodule. Formal Frontier Agents supplied the
intersection argument using Mathlib's flat preservation of injectivity,
extension-of-scalars maps and submodules (Kim Morrison, Johan Commelin and
Eric Wieser), and annihilator comparisons and suprema (Kenny Lau).
It transports annihilation through
linear images, without commuting arbitrary scalars or extending ideal map across
an infinite intersection. Its nonfinite free-module client uses Mathlib's
Finsupp and base-changed bases.

The arbitrary-module annihilator preservation and faithfully flat algebra
contraction formulas are Formal Frontier AI-agent contributions. They reuse
Mathlib's zero detection for tensor maps, credited to Judith Ludwig, Florent
Schaffhauser, Yunzhou Xie and Jujian Zhang, the tensor-map API credited to Kenny
Lau and Mario Carneiro, and Kenny Lau's annihilator restriction-scalars identity.
The generic argument applies zero detection to scalar endomorphisms; the algebra
formula then restricts the canonical tensor action along the algebra map.
These are source-independent auxiliary results, with no identified passage of
the background reference below.

## Tooling and background

Anchor adapted this project's [API adapter](../scripts/generate_api.py) from
earlier Formal Frontier work in algebraic-direct-limits, itself adapted from
ideal-completion, under the same original-project Apache-2.0 authorization.
The preserved historical adapter inventory covers seven mathematical leaves,
an aggregate and nine test modules; it binds 54 native library display sites to
source ranges and distinguishes 39 source docstrings from 15 authored API notes. The
[reference](API.md) contains those historical native signatures, docstrings and
notes rather than vendored documentation-site assets. The current
[square-zero](../CoherentModules/Algebra/TrivSqZeroExt/Finite.lean) and
[ideal/module transfer](../CoherentModules/RingTheory/Ideal/FiniteModuleTransfer.lean)
modules supply their own source documentation.

Lean, mathlib and doc-gen4 are independent tools or dependencies; their
authors and notices remain with their upstream projects and no dependency
website or source tree is distributed here. Mathematical background includes
Kazuhiro Fujiwara and Fumiharu Kato,
[*Foundations of Rigid Geometry I*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.4734v5),
and classical coherent-ring theory. No PDF, scan, substantial excerpt or
mathematical-source endorsement is bundled or claimed. Background citation
is not a complete formalization or rights clearance; release review separately
examines attribution, proof integrity and any concrete redistribution concerns.
