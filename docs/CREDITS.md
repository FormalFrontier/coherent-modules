## Mathematical work

Anchor contributed the original coherent-module foundation, kernel closure and
universal-coherence construction, coordinated integration, and wrote the
standalone guide. Other Formal Frontier AI-agent contributors developed the
finite-free kernel characterization, quotient and cokernel closure, coherent
module category, localization and descent, finite-presentation bridge and
exact-five criterion, extension closure, and Hom and tensor closure. The
finite square-zero kernel equivalence, whole-extension finiteness, projection
base change and finite-type ideal/module transfer, together with their zero and
torsion examples, are original Formal Frontier AI-agent contributions. The
tracked examples, aggregate imports and adaptations are collective project work.
The concise contributor lines in the Lean files credit that work
without treating pooled execution identifiers as human names. Original
revision-specific authorship and review records are maintained separately;
this document does not transfer credit to the integrator.

The faithful-flatness direct-sum criterion follows Mathlib's proper-ideal
criterion, flatness of dependent direct sums, tensor distribution, and
quotient-tensor equivalences. The component constructor reuses this library's
surjective linear-map theorem. The complementary ideals generated by 3 and 4
in `ZMod 6` follow the example in
[AlgebraicGroups](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/algebraic-groups), using
Mathlib's complement projections and projective splitting rather than
coalgebra constructions; this example introduces no dependency on that library.

The semilinear Noetherian-ascent results extend this library's ideal
unit-clearing argument using Mathlib's submodule comap and ascending-chain
criterion. Formal Frontier Agents developed the semimodule extension and the
one-sided refinement from units to scalars with left inverses. The
function-surjective corollary generalizes Mathlib's `isNoetherian_of_surjective`;
the range specialization documents agreement at its original hypotheses.
These are mathematical ideas and independently written proofs, not copied
source text or third-party assets.

The finite-generation clearing criteria combine this scalar-clearing family
with the library's semilinear span transport and Mathlib's finite-generation
and module-finiteness APIs. They use the target-scalar span of the comap image,
not a surjectivity assumption on the scalar homomorphism. The proofs and
sign-clearing example are original Formal Frontier AI-agent contributions.
